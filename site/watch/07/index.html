Confidence is not a probability

A confidence score is a recorded judgement, not a truth score. This note separates a direct observation from an inference and shows why the findings are never averaged.

format narrated MP4voice Hazel · documentarybase en-GBsource REA-20261121-afd06c73-83835

What this note covers

  • What the confidence number means.
  • Why findings are not averaged.
  • How the limit changes the claim.

What it is not

  • No probability claim.
  • No aggregate truth score.
  • No claim that confidence replaces a locator.

Confidence is not a probability.

Confidence is not a probability. It is a recorded judgement about the strength of an observation.

02
Ninety-five is direct.

Ninety-five is a direct observation with a strong locator. It still has a limit.

03

Eighty is strong, but it may depend on an inference. Say where that inference sits.

04

Sixty-five needs corroboration before it leads the story.

05
Do not average it.

Do not average the numbers into one truth score. One score hides the weak link.

06
Show the individual findings.

Show the individual findings, each with its own locator and limit.

07

Keep the limit attached. The number does not replace the evidence.

A number is not truth. Read the record behind it.
